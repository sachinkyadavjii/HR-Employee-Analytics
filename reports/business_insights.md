# Business Insights - HR Employee Analytics

These insights are derived directly from `data/hr_employee_data_clean.csv`
(1,200 synthetic employee records, 18.9% overall attrition rate). Each
insight includes the finding, the supporting evidence, a possible business
explanation, and an HR recommendation. Figures were generated by the
analysis in `notebooks/HR_Employee_Analysis.ipynb` and `sql/hr_queries.sql`
— none are invented.

---

## 1. Overtime is the strongest single attrition driver

**Finding:** Employees who regularly work overtime leave at more than double
the rate of those who don't.

**Evidence:** Employees on overtime have an attrition rate of **32.0%**
(111 of 347), compared to **13.6%** (116 of 853) for employees without
overtime.

**Possible Business Explanation:** Sustained overtime is a well-known driver
of burnout and reduced job satisfaction. Employees may see it as a sign of
understaffing or unclear workload expectations.

**HR Recommendation:** Audit teams/roles with chronic overtime, review
staffing levels, and consider workload redistribution or additional hiring
in the most overtime-heavy job roles and departments.

---

## 2. Which department has the highest attrition?

**Finding:** Human Resources has the highest attrition rate among the three
departments, followed by Research & Development.

**Evidence:** Human Resources: **22.3%** (33 of 148) — Research &
Development: **19.4%** (128 of 659) — Sales: **16.8%** (66 of 393).

**Possible Business Explanation:** Smaller departments can show more
volatile attrition rates with fewer employees, and HR roles are often
subject to demanding cross-functional workloads and emotional labor.

**HR Recommendation:** Conduct focused stay/exit interviews within HR and
R&D to identify department-specific pain points, rather than applying a
one-size-fits-all retention policy.

---

## 3. Which job roles are most affected?

**Finding:** HR Manager, Recruiter, and Research Director have the highest
attrition rates of any job role.

**Evidence:** HR Manager: **28.9%** (13 of 45) — Recruiter: **22.0%**
(13 of 59) — Research Director: **21.4%** (31 of 145).

**Possible Business Explanation:** These roles often carry high
responsibility, direct exposure to organizational change, and (for people
managers) emotional burden from managing others' careers and exits.

**HR Recommendation:** Prioritize retention conversations, career pathing,
and workload reviews for these specific roles rather than broad
department-wide initiatives.

---

## 4. Does salary level appear associated with attrition?

**Finding:** Employees who left earned meaningfully less, on average, than
those who stayed.

**Evidence:** Average monthly income of employees who left: **₹61,637**;
average monthly income of employees who stayed: **₹79,870** — a gap of
roughly 23%.

**Possible Business Explanation:** Employees who feel underpaid relative to
their role or the market are more likely to seek opportunities elsewhere,
especially when combined with other risk factors like overtime.

**HR Recommendation:** Run a compensation benchmarking review, particularly
for lower-paid employees in high-attrition roles, and consider structured
pay-progression paths tied to tenure and performance.

---

## 5. Are newer employees more likely to leave?

**Finding:** Yes. Employees in their first two years at the company leave at
more than three times the rate of employees with over 10 years of tenure.

**Evidence:** Employees with ≤2 years at the company: **36.7%** attrition;
employees with >10 years at the company: **10.4%** attrition. Average
tenure of employees who left is **6.6 years** versus **11.5 years** for
those who stayed.

**Possible Business Explanation:** New hires are still evaluating
job fit and are more exposed to onboarding gaps, unclear expectations, or a
mismatch between expectations set during hiring and daily reality.

**HR Recommendation:** Strengthen onboarding and the first 90 days
experience, assign mentors to new hires, and add structured check-ins at
30/60/90 days for employees in their first two years.

---

## 6. Does job satisfaction appear related to retention?

**Finding:** Attrition rate falls steadily as reported job satisfaction
rises.

**Evidence:** Job Satisfaction 1 (lowest): **32.6%** attrition — Level 2:
**21.7%** — Level 3: **16.1%** — Level 4 (highest): **13.7%**.

**Possible Business Explanation:** This is an intuitive but important
confirmation: dissatisfaction with day-to-day work is a leading indicator of
someone leaving, well before a resignation is submitted.

**HR Recommendation:** Introduce regular, lightweight pulse surveys to catch
dropping satisfaction early, and train managers to act on low scores with
one-on-one conversations rather than waiting for annual reviews.

---

## 7. Does lack of promotion appear associated with attrition?

**Finding:** Employees who have not received a promotion in the last 5 years
leave at a noticeably higher rate than those who have.

**Evidence:** Not promoted in last 5 years: **23.5%** attrition (107 of
456); promoted in last 5 years: **16.1%** attrition (120 of 744).

**Possible Business Explanation:** A lack of visible career progression is a
common driver of disengagement, particularly for ambitious or high-performing
employees who feel their growth has stalled.

**HR Recommendation:** Build clearer, more transparent promotion criteria
and career ladders, and flag employees who have gone multiple years without
a promotion for a career-development conversation.

---

## 8. Work-life balance and business travel also matter

**Finding:** Poor work-life balance and frequent business travel are both
associated with higher attrition.

**Evidence:** Work-life balance score 1-2 (poor): **23-25%** attrition vs.
**13.3%** for score 4 (best). Employees who travel frequently for business:
**24.5%** attrition vs. **15.9%** for those who don't travel.

**Possible Business Explanation:** Both factors reduce the time and energy
employees have for life outside work, which compounds over time into
burnout and disengagement.

**HR Recommendation:** Review travel policies for frequent travelers
(e.g. travel caps, compensating time off) and reinforce work-life balance
expectations with managers, especially in roles that combine overtime and
travel.

---

## Summary of Top Risk Factors (in order of observed impact)

1. Overtime (32.0% vs 13.6% attrition)
2. Tenure under 2 years (36.7% attrition)
3. Low job satisfaction (32.6% at the lowest level)
4. Lower income (23% gap between leavers and stayers)
5. Frequent business travel (24.5% attrition)
6. Poor work-life balance (23-25% attrition)
7. No recent promotion (23.5% attrition)

## Important Caveat

This dataset is **synthetic** and was generated with built-in relationships
between these factors and attrition (with added randomness so the
relationships are realistic rather than perfect). These insights demonstrate
the analytical workflow and are consistent with common real-world HR
research findings, but they should not be treated as findings about any
real company's workforce.
